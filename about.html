About Voile Kingdom

A small studio with a light touch

We design flowing, breathable clothing for warm days and long journeys, and we try to do it thoughtfully, one small collection at a time.

Where it began

It started with one dress that actually worked

Voile Kingdom began with a very ordinary problem. Every summer we bought clothes that looked beautiful in photos, and every summer they let us down: polyester that trapped the heat, linings that clung, and hems that fell apart after a few washes.

So we made our own. The first piece was a simple cotton voile dress with a tiered skirt, cut generously so air could move around it. People kept asking where it came from, and a small studio grew out of those conversations.

Our name is a nod to that first fabric. Voile is light, a little sheer and full of movement, and it still sets the tone for everything we make.

How we design

Fabric first, then the shape

Most brands start with a sketch and then look for a fabric to fill it. We work the other way round. A new collection begins with swatches pinned to the studio wall and a lot of draping on the stand to see how each cloth wants to fall.

Only once we understand the fabric do we start cutting patterns. Every sample is worn for real by our team, in the heat, on the subway, on a weekend away, before we decide whether it is good enough to make.

Plenty of ideas don’t make it. We would rather release six pieces we are proud of than thirty we are not sure about.

What we believe

Fewer, better, worn for longer

We are a small business and we won’t pretend to have solved fashion’s biggest problems. What we can do is make clothes that last, in quantities that make sense, and be open about how we work.

  • Natural fibres wherever possible. Linen, silk and cotton make up most of what we sell.
  • Small runs. Producing less means less waste and fewer end-of-season markdowns.
  • Honest descriptions. We tell you when a fabric is sheer, when it creases and how it should be washed.
  • Pieces that work together. Colours and shapes are planned so new pieces sit happily alongside older ones.
